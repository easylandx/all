The Brain's Keyhole

Visitors don't see your business. They see your page's version of it.

Every visitor hallucinates a version of your business from a handful of signals: load speed, layout, photos, the first sentence they read. If those signals are muddled, they don't dig deeper to find the "real" you — they just leave believing you're not worth the effort.

Magic thinking

Fake urgency, empty promises

  • Countdown timers that reset on refresh
  • "Best in the city!" with nothing to back it
  • Stock photos that could be any business
  • Vague calls to action — "Learn more"
Belief that forces action

Real signals, real next step

  • Real photos of the actual work or space
  • One specific, named action — "Book a fitting"
  • Visible proof: results, reviews, real clients
  • A page fast enough to not test their patience
The Three Powers, Applied

What a landing page controls in a visitor's mind

Three levers decide whether a visitor stays, trusts you, and clicks. A landing page built without them is asking the visitor to do all the believing on their own.

Attentionwhat they see

One focal point, not ten

A page with five competing banners forces the visitor's brain to filter for you. Give it one clear headline and one button, and there's nothing left to filter — the important thing is the only thing.

Anticipationwhat they feel

Design sets the price tag in their head

The same service on a cluttered, dated page feels cheap; on a clean, considered one, it feels worth paying for — before they've read a single word of your pricing.

Agencywhat they do

Make the next step feel possible

If booking looks like a five-step form, most visitors quietly decide it's not worth it — no matter how much they wanted your product a moment ago. One visible, one-tap action keeps their effort above zero.
