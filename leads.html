Six Ways to Open a Page

Matched to how well your visitor already knows you

A visitor discovering your business for the first time needs a different opening than one who already follows you and is ready to book. EasyLandX picks the right one for where your audience stands.

The Story Open

For visitors meeting your business for the first time

Drops the visitor straight into a moment from the business's world — a craftsperson at the bench, the first client who walked through the door, the spark that started it all. No pitch up front; the visitor arrives already caring.

On an EasyLandX page: a warm opening scene or short origin line above the fold, followed naturally by the offer once the visitor is already leaning in.

The Bold Statement Open

For visitors who scroll past most pitches

Opens with a confident, headline-worthy claim about the industry or the moment — the kind of line that reads like news rather than an advertisement, and earns a second look.

On an EasyLandX page: a striking, specific headline as the very first thing visible — big type, short sentence, zero small talk.

The Insider Open

For visitors who love feeling in the know

Teases a method, recipe, or approach that feels exclusive — the thing regular clients already know and new visitors are about to discover — before revealing what's on offer.

On an EasyLandX page: a "the technique behind this" or "what makes this different" section placed early, building curiosity before the booking button appears.

The Solution Open

For visitors actively looking for exactly this

Names precisely what the visitor is hoping to change or improve, shows real understanding of their situation, then presents the page as the clear next step.

On an EasyLandX page: a headline that mirrors the visitor's own goal in their own words, followed immediately by how the business delivers it.

The Promise Open

For visitors who know the category and want the payoff

Leads with the single biggest transformation the business delivers, then backs it up quickly with real proof — results, reviews, or a before-and-after.

On an EasyLandX page: a transformation-focused headline paired with a proof strip — testimonials, numbers, or photos — right below it.

The Offer Open

For visitors who already know and love the brand

Skips the build-up entirely. The price, the availability, the discount, or the booking link appears immediately — because the visitor already wants in and simply needs the door open.

On an EasyLandX page: the offer and the button sit at the very top, with everything else supporting a decision the visitor has already made.
