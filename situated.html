In Situated, Angela Duckworth shows that willpower is a limited resource — but environment is not. Design the surroundings well, and the right choice becomes the easy one, the default one, the one people take without a second thought.

The Situational Design Framework

Five moves EasyLandX makes to turn any landing page into an environment built for a "yes."

01 / AUDIT SITUATIONAL FRICTION

Find where visitors hesitate

Before we design a single pixel, we map the page like a floor plan — noting every point where a visitor's attention could wander or their momentum could slow. Every one of those points becomes an opportunity to smooth the path forward.

02 / MODIFY THE SITUATION

Make the right action the default

We restructure layout, hierarchy, and flow so that saying "yes" is the most obvious next step on the page — not one option competing among many.

03 / ADD DISTANCE FROM DISTRACTIONS

Give competing impulses less room

Extra menu items, unrelated offers, and busy navigation get trimmed back, so attention stays with the one goal that matters most on that page.

04 / CURATE SOCIAL PROOF

Show that others already said yes

Testimonials, client results, and trust markers are placed exactly where a visitor is deciding — a quiet, honest signal that this is a well-traveled, worthwhile path.

05 / DEFINE CLEAR ZONES

Give every section a single purpose

A hero that greets. A section that explains. A section that reassures. A section that invites action. Each zone does exactly one job, so visitors always know where they are and what happens next.
