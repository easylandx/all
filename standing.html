The skeleton is an emotional instrument

How a page carries itself tells a visitor whether to relax or brace.

Chen's core claim is that slumping or rigid bracing signals emergency to the nervous system before a single thought forms. A page does the same thing in the first half-second: cramped spacing and competing calls to action read as alarm, even if the offer underneath is solid.

EasyLandX designs the vertical first — one headline, one supporting line, one action — so the structure itself signals safety before anyone reads a word.

Collapsed page, visitor reaction:

"Too much going on — I don't trust this."

Aligned page, visitor reaction:

"This is clear. I know what to do next."

Tension is psychological armor

The parts of a page that grip hardest are usually the parts hiding doubt.

Chen finds chronic tightness in the jaw, shoulders, hips, and lower back mirrors suppressed emotion. On a page, the equivalent is over-explaining headlines, cluttered navigation, stacked competing buttons, and defensive fine print — all muscular gripping instead of structural trust.

Standing still is an active practice

Before shipping, run a Gravity Audit on the page.

Chen's "gravity audit" is a mid-stress pause: drop the shoulders, unclench the jaw, breathe down instead of up. EasyLandX runs the same pause on a page before launch — breathe through it, and check each section releases its grip.
