Five resets, five page decisions

Dr. Nerurkar's resets work through physical anchors rather than willpower, and micro-shifts rather than overhauls. The same logic applies to a landing page: small, deliberate choices that lower the effort of understanding you.

Reset 1

Get clear on what matters

The book asks you to name one Most Important Task each morning and protect your best hours for it.

On the page: one offer, one primary button, one job. Everything competing for that click moves off the page or below the decision.

Reset 2

Find rest in a restless world

Rest is an active biological process — repair, consolidation, balance — rather than idleness.

On the page: generous white space, short paragraphs, and visual pauses between sections so the eye can rest and keep reading.

Reset 3

Sync the body clock

Morning light and steady rhythm keep cortisol and melatonin predictable.

On the page: a predictable rhythm — promise, proof, price, action — repeated in the same order, so each scroll lands where the visitor expects.

Reset 4

Curb digital intake

A digital sunset and fewer alerts protect cognitive reserves from constant hyper-arousal.

On the page: quiet intake by design — no interrupting overlays, no autoplay, light assets, and a form asking only for what you truly need.

Reset 5

Shift the relationship with stress

Naming a sensation reduces the second layer of anxiety that sits on top of it.

On the page: microcopy that names the hesitation and answers it — what happens after the click, how long it takes, how easy it is to change course.

How an EasyLandX page gets built

  1. Name the page's Most Important Task

    One action, chosen before a word of copy is written. Calls, bookings and quotes each shape a different page, so the goal comes first.

  2. Give the content room to land

    Short blocks, real pauses, and a length matched to the decision. Rest on a page is active work, the same way it is in the body.

  3. Set a rhythm the visitor can follow

    Promise, proof, price, action — a steady sequence that makes the next scroll feel predictable and light to read.

  4. Keep the intake quiet

    Light assets, calm motion, a short form, and space left for the offer itself to be the loudest thing present.

  5. Answer the hesitation out loud

    Name what happens after the click. Naming the feeling settles it — on a page, that is microcopy doing quiet, measurable work.
