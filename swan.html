Two Worlds

Button-color tests live in Mediocristan. Breakout pages live in Extremistan.

Taleb split the world into Mediocristan, where no single data point moves the average much, and Extremistan, where one outlier dominates everything else combined. Landing pages sit in both at once — and confusing which world you're testing in is where most page-optimization programs quietly fail.

Mediocristan

Incremental page tweaks

  • Headline rewording, button color, form field order
  • Gains cluster in a narrow, predictable band
  • Safe to A/B test with modest traffic
  • Compounds slowly — worth doing, rarely worth headlines
Extremistan

The breakout page

  • A new offer, a new audience, a new format entirely
  • One page can outperform the other 400 combined
  • Impossible to forecast from past test data
  • You find it by shipping variants, not by refining one
The Turkey Problem

Your page converted beautifully for 999 days. Then traffic changed.

A page that performs well under steady, familiar traffic builds a false confidence — right up until a launch, a press mention, or a holiday sends a load and an audience it was never tested against. History of good performance is not evidence of future safety; it's evidence you haven't met your Thanksgiving yet.

Cognitive Traps

Three ways teams fool themselves about a page that worked

Exhibit B

The Narrative Fallacy

After a page converts well, everyone agrees on a tidy story for why. That story is usually invented after the fact — and it quietly becomes the template for the next twelve pages, whether or not it was ever the real cause.

Exhibit C

Silent Evidence

You see the competitor's page that took off. You never see the eleven near-identical pages they quietly killed first. Studying only the survivors makes any style look like a formula.

Exhibit D

The Ludic Fallacy

A clean dashboard with confidence intervals makes testing feel like a controlled game. The real market — real people, real devices, real timing — has no fixed rules and no capped downside.

The Barbell Strategy

Don't spread your pages evenly. Split them to the ends.

Put most of your pages on proven, safe designs — fast, tested, unlikely to embarrass you. Put a small slice of your effort into genuinely strange variants with almost nothing to lose and a real shot at the outlier. Skip the comfortable middle: the "slightly different" page that's expensive to build and too safe to break out.
