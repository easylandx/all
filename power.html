Leo Babauta's six laws, reapplied

How we build a page by removing, not adding

Zen Habits founder Leo Babauta built an entire philosophy on limiting yourself to the essential. It turns out that's also the whole job of a good landing page.

01

Set limitations

We cap every page to one headline, one offer, one call to action. No navigation maze, no five competing buttons.

02

Choose the essential

Before writing a word, we ask what the one thing is that a visitor absolutely must understand and do.

03

Simplify

Every paragraph, badge, and section has to earn its place. If it doesn't support the one goal, it gets cut.

04

Focus

A page that tries to be a blog, a shop, and a portfolio at once ends up being none of them well. Yours does one job, fully.

05

Start small

We launch one lean, working page first. Expand only once it's proven — never the other way around.

Your page's 3 MITs

Everything else waits until these are right

Babauta has people pick three Most Important Tasks before opening their inbox. Every EasyLandX page is built around its own three non-negotiables.

01

One clear headline and offer

Said in the first five seconds, in plain language — no jargon a visitor has to decode before deciding to stay.

02

One obvious next step

A single call to action, repeated, not competing with three other buttons pulling attention in different directions.

03

Just enough trust to act

One proof point — a result, a review, a guarantee — placed exactly where the hesitation would happen.

Our content rule

Keep, Combine, Defer, or Cut

Babauta clears his inbox with Do, Delegate, Defer, or Delete. We run every line of your existing website copy through the same filter before it earns a spot on your new page.

Keep

It directly supports the one goal of the page — it stays, unchanged.

Combine

Three overlapping sections saying the same thing become one, said well.

Defer

Good information, wrong page — it moves to a future page, not this one.

Cut

It doesn't move a visitor closer to the one action. It doesn't make the page.

The one-in, one-out rule

A page that never bloats back up

Babauta keeps his closet lean by never adding an item without removing one. We keep your page lean the same way — the day you want to add a new section, we ask which existing one it replaces.
