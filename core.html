Core thesis

Volume beats vision — but only if it moves fast

Do enough landing page attempts that it becomes unreasonable for one of them not to convert. A single hero, a single headline, a single offer will not tell you what's wrong. Ten will.

But raw output alone produces slop — a dozen near-identical pages that teach you nothing. The output has to be paired with velocity: the speed at which you publish, read the results, and adjust. Compress the gap between "we tried something" and "we know if it worked," and every page you ship afterward gets sharper. That compression is the entire reason easylandx exists — the faster the page goes live, the faster the market tells you the truth.

The frameworks

Rule of 100 & the velocity loop

Two disciplines: how much you ship, and how fast you learn from what you shipped.

01 — Volume

The Rule of 100

Commit to a brutal baseline: 100 landing page variants, headlines, or offer tests across 100 days. Not one perfect page you polish forever — a hundred honest attempts, each one a real market question instead of a guess made in private.

02 — Velocity

Velocity Made Good

Volume without speed just produces noise. Run the loop tight:

  • 1 Publish 100 raw page variants without overthinking any single one.
  • 2 Pull the top 10–20% by conversion and look closely.
  • 3 Isolate the exact line, layout, or offer detail that did the work.
  • 4 Rebuild future pages around only those winning details.
  • 5 Restart the loop faster than the round before.
The pitfall

Volume can't rescue a bad offer

!

More traffic to a weak page doesn't fix the page — it just multiplies how often it fails. If the pricing is confusing, the offer is vague, or the value isn't obvious in five seconds, shipping faster only shows more people something that doesn't work. Fix what the page is actually saying before you turn up the volume on who sees it.
