The robotic moment, applied to your website

People fall for a good-enough page — for about four seconds.

Research on human-robot bonding found that people will treat a machine as caring simply because it makes eye contact and asks a question back. Templated landing pages try the same trick: a countdown timer, a chat bubble, a stock photo of someone smiling at a laptop. It works for a moment, then a visitor feels the substitution and leaves.

Five ideas we build every page around

Reclaiming a little friction, on purpose.

The instinct in web design is to remove every point of friction until nothing is left to remember. We think a landing page should have exactly one point of friction: the decision you actually want your visitor to make.

scattered nav menu → one focused page

A sacred space for one decision

No distracting navigation, no five competing offers. Every element on the page points at a single action.

chatbot small talk → a real way in

Real contact, not a script

A phone number, a form that reaches a person, or a booking link — never a bot pretending to listen.

stock photo smiles → your actual business

Honest over polished

Real photos and plain language beat a stock image of a stranger, even when the stock image is prettier.

everything, everywhere → one job, done well

Monotasking, for pages

A landing page that sells one service converts better than a homepage trying to explain your whole company.

set once, forgotten → written for who's visiting
