The core thesis

Microcopy isn't decoration. It's the interface.

Kinneret Yifrah's four pillars, translated into what actually gets typed on an EasyPageX landing page.

01

Be human

No robotic system-speak. Every label reads like a friendly shopkeeper greeting a customer at the door.

Not: "Submission Error."
We write: "Oops — looks like that email's missing an @. Mind fixing it?"
02

Make everything clear

We answer the doubt before the visitor even feels it — right next to the button or field that caused it.

Not: a bare phone field.
We write: "We only text your booking confirmation. No spam, promise."
03

Motivate action

CTAs state the reward, not the mechanism. "Click Here" tells a user nothing about what happens next.

Not: "Submit."
We write: "Reserve my table" / "Send my quote request"
04

Make the journey enjoyable

Loading spinners and thank-you screens get a bit of personality — small delight, big memory.

Not: "Loading…"
We write: "Warming up the espresso machine…"

Practical takeaways

Every EasyPageX page starts with a mini voice guide

Before we write a single button label, we lock in three things.

01

Define the character

Is your brand the warm neighborhood café, or the precise, no-nonsense clinic? We pick words that match — consistently, on every screen.

02

Clarity beats cleverness

A pun that confuses a visitor costs you a booking. If a clever line makes the next step unclear, we simplify it — every time.

03

Write for a glance, not a read

Visitors scan, they don't study. Short, scannable, precise — so the next logical action is always obvious.
