Why this matters

The comfort trap

Researchers who study how people relate to AI companions have flagged the same pattern again and again: systems that never push back, never get tired, and never ask for anything in return feel comfortable in the moment — but they teach us to expect relationships without friction. A website can fall into the same trap. Auto-playing videos, countdown timers that reset when you refresh, chat pop-ups that won't leave you alone — all of it is designed to keep you scrolling, not to help you decide. It works for engagement. It's terrible for trust.

A small business doesn't need a visitor who scrolls forever. It needs one who reads, understands, and picks up the phone. That's a different design problem, and it's the one we solve.

Our approach

Five habits of a page people actually trust

Click each one — this is roughly the order we work through on every project.

  • Before we design anything, we read your current site the way a skeptical customer would. Every claim, every button, every piece of friction gets questioned: does this help someone decide, or does it just fill space?

  • No sidebar of ten competing offers, no five different fonts fighting for attention. One page, one purpose, one obvious next step — the way a good conversation has one topic at a time.

  • Polished stock-photo testimonials read as fake because they usually are. We use your actual customers' words, even when they're a little rough — real friction reads as real trust.

  • A form that asks a real question, a price that's actually visible, a "call us" button that goes to a person — small moments of effort that prove there's something genuine on the other end.

  • No exit-intent popups guilting people into staying. A page that respects a visitor's pace earns a better decision than one that panics them into a click.
