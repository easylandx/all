Two ways to see a landing page

Psychologist Carol Dweck found that people either see ability as fixed or as something built through effort. The same split shows up in how businesses see their landing pages.

The fixed view

"The page is the verdict"

  • One launch is treated as the final answer on whether the idea works.
  • A quiet first week reads as proof the product, or the market, isn't right.
  • Changing the page feels like admitting the first version was a mistake.
The growth view

"The page is a project"

  • Every launch produces information — a signal about what to try next.
  • A quiet first week is data, not a verdict, and it points to a specific next move.
  • Refining the page is exactly the plan, not a departure from it.

How we work with every business

A repeatable, four-step rhythm behind every page we build and refine.

1

We launch it honestly

The first version goes live as a genuine starting point, not a finished statement — so what it teaches us is real.

2

We watch the real signals

Scroll depth, time on page, click paths, and drop-off points tell us far more than a single conversion number can.

3

We name exactly what's holding it back

A specific, nameable sticking point — a headline, a proof gap, a slow section — replaces any vague sense that "something's off."

4

We answer with a growth move

One targeted change goes live, and the next round begins with a page that's already a little stronger.
