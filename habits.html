The golden rule of habit change

Don't fight the visitor's habit. Redirect it.

You can't make a distracted, skeptical visitor into a patient reader. Duhigg's research shows the only habits that actually change are the ones where the cue and the reward stay put, and only the routine in between gets swapped for something easier.

Old routine

Read three paragraphs, find the form, fill six fields, wait

Same cue that brought them in. Same reward they were promised. But the routine between the two is long enough that most visitors quietly leave before they reach it.

Keystone habits

One page can move your whole funnel

Duhigg's research on organizations found that certain habits — a daily workout, a nightly checklist — act as keystones: change that one behavior and disciplines you never touched start improving on their own. A landing page can work the same way inside a business.

Fix the page visitors land on first, and the effect spreads

Teams that rebuild their primary landing page with EasyLandX report the same ripple pattern: the ad copy upstream tightens to match it, the follow-up email downstream starts quoting its language, and the sales call finally opens with the same promise the page made. One keystone page, rebuilt once, resets the habits around it.
