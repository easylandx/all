The discipline

Three rules we run every page through

Before we design a single pixel, every easylandx page is filtered through the same three questions journalists and researchers have used for decades to separate real signal from polite noise.

Rule 01

Speak to their life, not our template

A page copied from a template talks about the business. A page that converts talks about the visitor's Tuesday afternoon — the exact moment they searched for a fix.

Rule 02

Build on what happened, not what might

We don't ask "would you like this?" We ask what you're doing right now, what it's costing you, and what you've already tried. Hypotheticals don't build good headlines. History does.

Rule 03

Listen longer than we design

The best line on the page is rarely one we wrote first. It's usually something a real customer said, almost word for word, that we simply gave a home.

Diagnostic

The question you ask decides the page you get

Small business owners get asked "do you like it?" constantly — by friends, by builders, by their own team. That question is almost useless. Here's what we ask instead, and why it changes what ends up on the page.

Homepage headline
Common
"Do you think a nicer website would help your business?"
easylandx
"Where do most customers drop off right now — the DM, the call, or the price?"
// The first invites a polite "sure." The second names the exact sentence that has to go on the page.
Pricing & offer
Common
"What would you pay for a page like this?"
easylandx
"What are you spending right now trying to get customers — ads, a builder, a freelancer?"
// Hypothetical prices are invented on the spot. Existing spend tells us the problem is real and already funded.
Feature requests
Common
"Add a chat widget, a countdown, and a video — just in case."
easylandx
"Walk me through the last customer you actually lost. What happened right before that?"
// A feature request is a guess at a fix. The story behind it is the actual problem worth solving.
In practice

How this shows up in the build

i.

Keep the first call casual, not a pitch

We don't open a discovery call with "let's review your requirements." We open with "tell me what happened the last time someone almost bought from you, and didn't." Defenses stay down. The real answers come out.

ii.

Chase the reason, not the request

When a client asks for a specific section, banner, or badge, we ask why — once, sometimes twice. Often the real fix is a clearer sentence higher up the page, not another element competing for attention.

iii.

Design the page to earn a real action

A page succeeds when someone books a call, sends a deposit, or replies with a real question — not when someone says "looks nice." Every easylandx page is built around one clear, specific action, not a vague "learn more."
