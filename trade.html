The ties that bind — and could unravel

A landing page built from Ed Conway's Trade World: the hidden supply chains behind everyday life, and the strategy playbook for a more fragmented world.

Core messages

Why the world runs on things you can't see

Trade isn't background noise to modern life — it's the skeleton holding it up. Conway's book maps how that skeleton was built for speed, not strength.

The invisible skeleton

Every device you own passes through dozens of countries before it reaches your hand — raw material, refinement, assembly, shipping — mostly unseen.

Efficiency vs. resilience

Just-in-time logistics squeezed out every spare buffer. Lean is cheap, until one delay anywhere becomes a shortage everywhere.

Weaponized interdependence

Ports, pipelines and processing plants aren't neutral anymore. They're leverage — used deliberately in trade disputes and standoffs.

The great fragmentation

Open global markets are giving way to friend-shoring and resource nationalism. Rebuilding at home is necessary — and slow, and costly.

Essential lessons

What the last decade of shocks taught us

  • 1

    Choke points are global vulnerabilities

    The Strait of Malacca, the Suez Canal, the Taiwan Strait — a handful of narrow points carry outsized risk for the whole system. See the map below.

  • 2

    Physical trade still rules

    Software and finance get the headlines, but rare earths, cargo ships and refineries are still what keep the lights on.

  • 3

    De-globalizing has a price

    Onshoring builds resilience, but it also means higher costs, slower innovation, and new competition over the same raw materials.

Framework

Conway's resilience playbook, step by step

Tap a step to open it.

1Map the physical bottlenecks▾

Audit supply chains down to raw material sources — not just Tier-1 suppliers, but where the lithium, cobalt and copper actually come from.

2Shift to "just-in-case"▾

Build strategic reserves and diversify freight routes, so one blocked lane doesn't stall the whole operation.

3Deploy friend-shoring▾

Diversify source markets across geopolitically aligned, stable partners rather than one region.

4Invest in material refining▾

Onshore critical processing and manufacturing steps, closing the gap between raw material and finished part.

5Design for recyclability▾

Circular models — battery recycling, e-waste recovery — turn domestic waste into a strategic reserve of its own.
