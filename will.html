Part 1

The myth of the "convincing" landing page

Most businesses try to write their way to a sale — more persuasive copy, more urgency, more exclamation points. That's asking the visitor's willpower to do the work. It won't. Environment always wins.

Attention is a finite resource

A visitor's focus depletes with every scroll, every pop-up, every second of load time. By the third distraction, they're gone — no matter how good your offer is.

Internal conflict kills conversion

"Is this legit? Where do I even click? What happens next?" Every unanswered question is friction fighting against the sale you're trying to make.

Your page is the environment

You can't out-write a bad structure. If the layout is chaotic, no headline will save it. Fix the environment first — the words come second.

Part 2

Designing a high-performance page

A landing page needs distinct zones, just like a well-designed life: one built to drive action, one built to build trust — never both fighting for attention at once.

Focus zone

The hero

One message, one action, zero competition. This is where we push for the click — nothing else lives here.

  • Single clear headline
  • One primary button
  • No distracting menus above the fold
Trust zone

The proof

Where visitors slow down to verify you're real: reviews, photos, results — calm, credible, unhurried.

  • Real client results
  • Photos over stock imagery
  • Direct contact links, always visible

Part 3

Forcing functions, built into the page

A forcing function removes the choice not to act. On a landing page, that's not a trick — it's just good design that makes the next step obvious and easy.

01

The button follows the visitor

A sticky call-to-action means there's never a moment where taking action requires scrolling back to find it.

02

One path, not five

Every link on the page leads toward the same outcome — booking, buying, or messaging you. No competing exits.

03

Direct line, no forms to abandon

A WhatsApp or Instagram button replaces a contact form nobody finishes. Say hello in one tap.

Implementation intentions, applied to your business

If you run a wellness or coaching practice Then your page opens with a booking button, not a biography.
If you sell food or handmade goods Then the menu or catalog is one scroll away, with an order link on every section.
If you run a pet, local, or community service Then a map, hours, and a message button sit above the fold — no digging required.
