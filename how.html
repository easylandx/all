The Evidence Board

Six triggers, filed and cross-referenced

Every "compliance professional" — salespeople, marketers, negotiators — leans on one of these six shortcuts. Pin them to your landing page and watch hesitation turn into action.

Exhibit 01 / Reciprocity

The Debt of a Gift

The Rule

People feel a strong pull to repay whatever they've been given.

The Trigger

Offer something genuinely useful first, before you ever ask for anything.

The Playbook

Open big, then step back to a smaller ask — the concession itself feels like a gift.

Exhibit 02 / Commitment

The Straight Line

The Rule

Once someone takes a stance, they'll work hard to act consistently with it.

The Trigger

Win a small, easy "yes" now — it quietly paves the way for a bigger one later.

The Playbook

Get a foot in the door with a tiny first step, then build compliance one stage at a time.

Exhibit 03 / Social Proof

The Crowd's Compass

The Rule

When unsure how to act, people copy whatever everyone else is already doing.

The Trigger

Show the crowd. Numbers, faces, and peer behavior settle doubt faster than any argument.

The Playbook

Lead with testimonials, star ratings, and usage counts that make "everyone else" visible.

Exhibit 04 / Liking

The Familiar Face

The Rule

A "yes" comes far easier from someone we already like.

The Trigger

Build warmth through shared ground, sincere compliments, and visible good looks or effort.

The Playbook

Find the common bond and hand out real praise early — rapport closes doors that logic can't.

Exhibit 05 / Authority

The Weight of a Title

The Rule

People defer to experts and figures of status with barely a second thought.

The Trigger

Signal power plainly — credentials, uniforms, tailored presentation, and clear status markers.

The Playbook

Put the expertise on the table before the ask, so trust is already earned by the time you request it.

Exhibit 06 / Scarcity

The Closing Door

The Rule

Whatever is running out feels instantly more valuable.

The Trigger

Frame the moment around what's lost by waiting, not just what's gained by acting.

The Playbook

Put a clock or a count on the offer — urgency does the persuading that features can't.
