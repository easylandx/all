A theory of landing pages

The words on your page aren't the message. The page is.

Swap the headline, swap the photo, swap the offer — a landing page still does the same job underneath: move a stranger down one path, at one speed, toward one button. That structure is what turns a visitor into a customer, long before anyone finishes reading.

Every medium rearranges behavior before it says a word

A lightbulb has no headline, no argument, no plot — and it still reorganized the entire day around it, inventing the night shift along with it. A landing page works the same way. Before a visitor reads your first sentence, the structure has already decided where their eye lands, how many decisions they're asked to make, and how far they scroll before there's anything to click.

Static medium

The brochure

  • Everything explained at once
  • No single next step
  • Reader sets the pace, then leaves
  • Built to be read
Active medium

The landing page

  • One idea per screen
  • One button, repeated on cue
  • The page sets the pace
  • Built to be clicked

What the structure is actually doing

This is the part visitors never notice consciously — and the part that decides whether they stay.

01 · LOAD

A page that opens in under two seconds hasn't lost anyone yet. Every extra second is a door quietly closing.

02 · SCROLL

One idea per screen. The scroll becomes the argument, instead of a wall of text trying to make the whole case at once.

03 · TRUST

A review, a real photo, a number — placed exactly where doubt shows up, not buried on a page nobody visits.

04 · MOBILE

Most visitors arrive on a phone, already moving. The page has to work like it knows that.

05 · ACTION

One button, worded the same way everywhere it appears — so clicking it never feels like a new decision.
