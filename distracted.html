The Core Mismatch

Ancient Hardware

Attention, working memory, and goal management evolved for a far slower environment — and have changed very little since. Modern devices exploit that gap.

High-Speed Triggers

Endless notifications and infinite feeds are built to exploit our drive to seek novel information — pulling focus away, one ping at a time.

The Multitasking Myth

The brain cannot run two attention-demanding tasks at once. What feels like multitasking is really rapid task-switching — and every switch carries a cost: slower work, more errors, and drained mental energy. A page (or a workday) that asks for split attention is asking for worse output.

Distraction vs. Interruption

Interference comes in two forms. Drag each card into the zone where it belongs.

Sort the Interference

Distraction

Stimuli you filter out while staying on task.

Interruption

A decision point where you pivot away entirely.

The Foraging Theory of Technology

Animals forage for food by weighing cost against reward. Humans now forage for information the same way. High technology makes the "cost" of a quick dopamine hit nearly zero — so we abandon deep work early to check email, social media, or news, again and again.

Essential Lessons

Goal Encroachment

Every interruption costs real time and energy to rebuild full focus on the original task.

Tech Anxiety

FOMO and unread-message dread trigger compulsive checking — keeping the nervous system in low-grade stress.

Metacognition First

Simply noticing your brain seeking novelty lets you intervene before the habit loop takes over.

Hardware Can Grow

Exercise, mindfulness, nature, and cognitive training all strengthen cognitive control over time.

  • 30–45 min focus blocks, then a deliberate 5-minute break.
  • Turn off non-essential notifications; put the phone in another room.
  • 30 minutes of daily cardio to boost neuroplasticity.
  • 10–15 minutes of daily breath-awareness practice.
  • Regular walks in nature to restore attentional capacity.
  • Set clear "when I'm reachable" expectations with others.
