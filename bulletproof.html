Three ideas that change how a landing page gets made

The method behind this page comes from a book on structured problem solving. EasyLandX applies it to one job: turning visitors into customers.

Problem solving is a learnable skill

A page that converts is the result of a method, not a flash of genius. EasyLandX runs the same process for every client, so quality does not depend on a lucky day.

Avoid solution-first thinking

Choosing colors and writing headlines before you know the real problem is how pages end up answering the wrong question. We frame the problem first and design second.

Structure fosters creativity

A logic tree does not box ideas in. It points creative energy at the few places on a page where it moves results.

    Key strategies and tactics

    Three habits that keep the work honest

    The 7 steps give the route. These habits keep the team on it when a project gets messy.

    Porpoising

    Move between the big picture and the fine detail. Check the goal, then test one headline, then check the goal again. It stops a team from polishing a button on a page that is aimed at the wrong thing.

    Debiasing

    Your own mind is the first thing to challenge. Open each one to see the trap and the guard.

    Confirmation bias

    You fall in love with the first headline, so you only notice the numbers that back it up.

    Guard: Before a test starts, write down the result that would prove you wrong.

    Anchoring bias

    The first layout or price you saw becomes the yardstick for every option after it.

    Guard: Sketch three different page directions before choosing one.
