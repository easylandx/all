03 · Mental shortcuts, applied to pages

Four biases every visitor brings to your page. easylandx designs around them, honestly.

System 2 is lazy — it prefers to trust System 1's shortcuts rather than do the work itself. That's not a flaw to exploit; it's a constraint to design for.

WYSIATI

What's above the fold is the whole pitch

Visitors build a full story from whatever's visible first — they don't scroll looking for missing context. easylandx hero blocks front-load the complete value proposition, not a teaser for it.

Anchoring

The first price sets the scale for every price after it

Whatever number a visitor sees first becomes the yardstick for everything that follows. easylandx pricing blocks let you choose which plan anchors the page, deliberately.

Availability

Vivid proof outweighs average proof

One specific, named testimonial with a real result feels more convincing than a stat sheet. easylandx proof sections are built around a small number of vivid, specific stories rather than a wall of logos.

Substitution

"Should I buy this?" becomes "Do I like this button?"

Faced with a hard question, visitors quietly answer an easier one. easylandx keeps the easy question and the real answer aligned, so a good gut feel about the page is also a good signal about the product.

04 · How visitors weigh risk

People don't weigh outcomes. They weigh the frame you put around them.

Prospect Theory says a loss hurts roughly twice as much as an equivalent gain feels good — and the same fact, framed two ways, produces two different decisions. Landing page copy is where this plays out line by line.

Loss framing
Copy pattern"Only 3 spots left at this price."
Why it worksThe fear of losing the current price outweighs the appeal of getting it.

05 · What visitors remember

They don't remember the whole visit. They remember the peak and the end.

Kahneman's Peak-End Rule says an experience is judged by its most intense moment and how it finished — not its length or its average. A landing page isn't remembered scroll-by-scroll. It's remembered by its best moment and its last one.

The dip in the middle — a slow-loading section, a confusing pricing table — matters far less than one strong peak and a confident close.

The experiencing visitor

Feels the page in real time — every scroll, every load delay, every confusing label. easylandx keeps this self comfortable: fast loads, clear hierarchy, no dead ends.

The remembering visitor

Decides whether to buy, bookmark, or share based on one peak moment and the final screen. easylandx gives every page one deliberate peak — a demo, a result, a proof point — and a closing section built to be the last thing they see, not an afterthought.

easylandx gives every page a fast, honest hero for the gut check, and a well-ordered body for the follow-up questions — so the page that convinces someone in six seconds is the same one that holds up under a slow read.
