The Attention Map

Every element on a page earns its place — or it goes.

Before we design anything, we sort every idea a business wants on its page into four groups. Only two of them make it to launch.

Keep — Purposeful

The headline that names the offer

Attractive and productive: the hero message, the price, the one button. This is what a visitor came for.

Keep — Necessary

Speed, clarity, mobile fit

Unglamorous but load-bearing: fast load times, readable type, a page that works on any phone screen.

Set Aside — Scattering

The auto-playing video

Attractive but unproductive: animations, pop-ups, and menus that pull eyes away from the one action that matters.

Set Aside — Excess

The extra menu tab

Unattractive and unproductive: filler pages and stock copy that add clicks between a visitor and a decision.

The Build Ritual

How we get a page from idea to hyperfocus.

The same four moves, every time, so nothing pulls attention away from the goal you hired the page to do.

01

Choose the one action

Book a call, place an order, send a message — we pick the single outcome the page exists to drive, before a single pixel is placed.

02

Clear the distractions

We remove the extra menus, the auto-playing media, the competing offers — anything that could split a visitor's attention.

03

Build toward that one action

Every section — headline, proof, pricing — leads a visitor forward, in order, toward the button that matters.

04

Bring wandering eyes back

A floating action button keeps the one next step in view, gently, wherever a visitor scrolls to.
