A Theory of Landing Pages

There's no perfect landing page. Only a better one.

Twenty-four centuries of moral philosophy keep arriving at the same conclusion: perfection was never the point. Trying, falling short, and improving is what actually moves things forward. We built EasyLandX on the same idea — for pages, not people.

The Signature Idea

The Golden Mean of a landing page

Aristotle placed every virtue between two failures. Courage sits between cowardice and recklessness. A landing page has its own mean — between saying too little and saying too much.

Deficiency
A blank hero, no headline, no button. The visitor guesses and leaves.
Golden Mean
One message, one proof point, one action. Nothing missing, nothing extra.
Excess
Three popups, a chatbot, and a countdown timer before the headline loads.
Part One

Three ways to judge a page

Moral philosophy tests an action against three big frameworks before calling it good. We test a landing page the same way.

Virtue

The Golden Mean

Aristotle argued that character is built by finding the sweet spot between extremes. EasyLandX pages are deliberately built in that middle — enough to convince, nothing to trip over.

Duty

The Non-Negotiables

Kant believed some rules hold regardless of outcome. We hold a few, too: your price is never hidden, your form never sells your inbox, your load time is never sacrificed for a stock photo.

Outcome

What Actually Works

Utilitarians measured goodness by results. A landing page's only real virtue is whether a visitor acts. Every layout and headline earns its place by that single test.

Part Two

Where it gets complicated

As choices scale from one page to a whole web presence, the simple rules start to strain.

Contractualism

What we owe every visitor

The philosopher T.M. Scanlon asked what rules no reasonable person could object to. Applied to the web: a page that loads fast, that says what it's selling before it asks for anything, that never traps a visitor in a popup with no way out. That's the deal, before a single word of copy.

Ubuntu

A page is a page through other people

The Southern African philosophy of Ubuntu holds that a person becomes a person only through other people. A landing page works the same way — a headline alone rarely convinces anyone. It's the testimonial, the client logo, the proof that someone else already trusted this, that finishes the sentence.

Part Three

How to fail better

Try. Fail. Fail better. That's the whole method — for a moral life, and for a landing page.

Moral Luck

Luck isn't skill

A page that converts once might just be lucky traffic. A page that converts consistently is designed well. We build for the second kind, not the first.
