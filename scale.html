The three laws

The mathematics of growth, translated into page design.

Three regimes govern every growing system. Two are worth chasing. One is worth outgrowing. Here's how each becomes a rule for how we build your page.

I exponent ≈ 0.75
Sublinear · Biology

The Efficiency Law

In nature

Kleiber's Law: as an organism grows, its energy use grows slower than its mass. A gram of elephant tissue runs calmer and more efficiently than a gram of mouse tissue.

On your page

One self-contained file. No plugin stack, no bloated framework. As we add sections, testimonials, and interactions, your page stays light — it gets calmer, not heavier.

II exponent ≈ 1.15
Superlinear · Cities

The Connection Law

In cities

Every time a city's population doubles, wages, patents, and ideas rise by roughly 15% per resident. Density turns people into a network that multiplies output.

On your page

Each section is placed to compound the one before it — proof stacks on proof, until every visitor has more paths to "yes" than the layout alone would suggest.

III exponent → below 1
Corporate plateau

The Law We Design Around

In companies

As headcount grows, administration grows faster than output. Innovation is replaced by process, and most corporations settle into a slow, sublinear decline.

On your page

Generic builders follow the same curve: more settings, more menus, more overhead for you to manage. EasyLandX skips the org chart — one person, one file, built by hand for your business.
