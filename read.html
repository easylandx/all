The truth is in the body.

The book teaches that spoken words are easy to manage, while posture, micro-expressions and movement are far harder to fake. For landing pages, scrolls, clicks, hesitations and exits are the body language of a visit. EasyLandX turns these four ideas into design.

01

Read clusters

One gesture proves nothing. One bounce means little; a bounce plus fast scroll plus no click is a cluster. We design around patterns.

02

Check congruence

Do your promises match the page behavior? Headline says “simple”, form asks twenty fields: that contradiction breaks trust.

03

Add context

Crossed arms may just mean cold. Mobile, traffic source, culture and timing change what a signal means.

04

Mirror and adjust

Know when to push forward, when to back off and when to close. Pages and apps adapt in real time.

Signals, translated.

Open vs defensive

Open palms, uncrossed legs: cooperation. Crossed arms, angled away: doubt. On a page: clear pricing and risk-free trials open people up.

Evaluating

Chin stroke, head tilt: weighing a proposal. Give comparison tables and proof, then leave space.

Bored

Tapping, looking away: move on. Shorten the path and show the payoff sooner.

Suspicion

Nose touch, avoided eye contact: hidden doubt. Add honest answers, real names and visible guarantees.

Confidence vs nerves

Firm posture reads as authority; fidgeting reads as anxiety. Calm, confident layouts reassure nervous buyers.
