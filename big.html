The five moves, applied to a landing page

The EasyLandX build sequence

This is a genuine sequence: each step earns the speed of the one after it.

1

Think slow: prototype in words and wireframes

Before a single line of code, your page exists as a written offer, a headline set, a proof list, and a grey-box wireframe. Sketches are cheap and endlessly editable, so this is where ideas get stress-tested, swapped, and sharpened. By the time the layout is agreed, the interesting decisions are already made.

Iterate here, where it costs the least
2

Anchor the estimate in pages already live

Reference class forecasting means your timeline comes from the outcomes of similar pages that actually shipped, rather than from a hopeful best case. A restaurant page with a menu, a gallery, and a reservation flow inherits the real numbers from restaurant pages built before it. Your quote and your calendar start from evidence.

Your page belongs to a class with a track record
3

Design for modularity

Every EasyLandX page is assembled from a kit of proven sections: hero, offer, proof, gallery, FAQ, contact, booking. Each module has been built, tested, and measured many times over, so adding one is a known quantity — predictable hours, predictable behaviour on mobile, predictable performance scores.

Repeatable parts, reliable totals
4

Hand the work to people who have built it before

Experience is the most reliable ingredient in the whole study. EasyLandX builds one thing — conversion-focused landing pages for small businesses — and builds it repeatedly. Your project gets a team that already knows how this exact page behaves on a phone, in an ad click-through, and in search results.

A specialist team, on familiar ground
5

Act fast: a short, focused build window

With the thinking finished and the modules chosen, construction becomes assembly. The build window stays compressed, which keeps the whole project inside a stable stretch of your business calendar and gets your page earning as early as possible.
