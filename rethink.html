The core problem

Picturing your launch feels like progress. Your brain agrees — and stops trying.

Dr. Gabriele Oettingen's research on motivation found that vividly visualizing a successful outcome, on its own, lowers the energy needed to actually produce it. Applied to landing pages, the pattern is familiar.

The relaxation trap

"I can already see it live."

You picture the finished page, the traffic rolling in, the boss impressed by the numbers. It feels good — good enough that your body quietly treats the goal as handled. The urgency to actually open the editor fades.

Mental contrasting

"Here's what's actually stopping me."

Hold that same vision next to the real obstacle in your way — not the market, not the tool, but the internal one: the perfectionism, the fear of a bad first draft. That contrast is what generates real energy to act.

WOOP, applied to landing pages

Four steps that turn "I should launch a page" into a page that's live.

WOOP pairs an optimistic wish with a sober obstacle, then automates the response — so execution doesn't depend on willpower on the day itself.

W
Wish

A specific, realistic goal with a deadline attached.

"I want a live landing page for the new offer by Friday."
O
Outcome

The best-case result, felt vividly.

"Signups come in overnight and my manager notices the page is finally live."
O
Obstacle

The internal thing that actually stalls you — not the market.

"I keep polishing the layout because I'm afraid it looks unfinished."
P
Plan

An if-then rule that fires automatically, no willpower required.

"IF I catch myself tweaking spacing again, THEN I publish the page as-is within 30 minutes."
