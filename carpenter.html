The five moves, applied to a landing page build

The EasyLandX craft method

Ellison's framework was built for wood, stone, and metal. It transfers to a browser almost without translation.

1

Deconstruct the impossible

Before any design begins, your page gets broken into its true components: headline, offer, proof, gallery, contact, and a handful more depending on your business. Each brick is small enough to build with precision, and finishing one clarifies exactly what the next one needs.

2

Audit the system's real constraints

Wood has grain; a business has a budget, a timeline, and a set of photos that actually exist. We measure your real material — your assets, your voice, your calendar — before drawing anything, so the plan works with what you actually have rather than an imagined ideal version of it.

3

Track the tolerances that compound

A sixteenth of an inch off at the base becomes inches of drift at the top of a wall. On a page, a headline that's slightly off-target, a photo that loads slowly, a price that's unclear — small deviations that seem minor on their own, compounding into a visitor who leaves before deciding. We re-check alignment against your goal at every stage, not just at the end.

4

Build in adaptive correction

Plans shift: a photo doesn't work, a section needs reordering, a launch date moves. The measure of the build isn't a plan that never changes — it's the calm, structural fix that keeps the whole thing sound. EasyLandX treats every adjustment as a joinery problem with a solution, not a crisis.

5

Invest in the hidden framing

What a visitor never consciously notices — load speed, mobile behaviour, clean code, accessible structure — is exactly what holds the visible page up. EasyLandX puts the same care into that substructure as into the parts you'll actually look at, because surface polish is only as stable as what's underneath it.

Material realism

Design around what's actually there, not the idealized version

Ellison stopped expecting wood to behave like something it isn't. EasyLandX applies the same audit to a small business's real starting material.

"We need a full photo shoot before we start."→We build with the photos you have today and improve them as you grow.
"Our copy needs to be perfect first."→We draft from how you already talk about your business, then refine it.
"We should wait for the busy season to pass."→We build around your real calendar, including the busy weeks.
"The page has to do everything at once."→One page, one job: turn a visit into a decision. Everything else can be a later addition.
